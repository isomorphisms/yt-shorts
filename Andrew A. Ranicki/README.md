# Andrew A. Ranicki — posted book PDFs

Source archive: [Andrew Ranicki's University of Edinburgh books page](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/index.htm).

This folder is a source/research index. It does **not** copy the books into this repository and it does not yet propose videos. The point is to preserve credit, point back to the original PDFs, and record what each document actually contains.

## 1. The geometric Hopf invariant and surgery theory

- **Authors:** Michael Crabb and Andrew Ranicki
- **Date:** 2017/2018
- **Length:** 420 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/hopfsurgery.pdf
- **arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.08832

This book joins two lines of work that Crabb and Ranicki had pursued since the 1970s: geometric questions about double points of maps of manifolds, and algebraic/quadratic structures in homotopy and surgery theory. Its central object is the **geometric Hopf invariant** of a stable map. Roughly, that invariant measures the failure of a stable map to behave like an unstable map with respect to diagonal maps.

The geometric side is especially concrete: for an immersion, the book relates the Hopf invariant to the actual double-point set. The algebraic side identifies the same construction with the quadratic construction used in Ranicki's algebraic surgery theory. The book then uses that bridge to reinterpret Wall's double-point invariant, ordinary surgery obstructions, and Ranicki's total surgery obstruction. It is a long synthesis of stable homotopy theory, equivariant homotopy, immersion theory, bordism, and algebraic surgery rather than a narrow book about the classical Hopf invariant alone.

## 2. Exotic Homology Manifolds — Oberwolfach 2003

- **Editors:** Frank Quinn and Andrew Ranicki
- **Published:** Geometry & Topology Monographs, 2006; workshop held in 2003
- **Length:** 158 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/homproc.pdf

Proceedings of a small Oberwolfach workshop devoted to **homology manifolds**, especially "exotic" examples. The preface explains the meeting as a deliberate encounter between the point-set-topology tradition and the algebraic/geometric surgery tradition. A major focus was the proof that exotic homology manifolds actually exist and, in particular, the stability or "shrinking" step needed in that construction.

The volume includes J. Bryant on homologically arc-homogeneous ENRs; Daverman and Halverson on codimension-one manifold factors; Hegenbarth and Repovš on the Bryant–Ferry–Mio–Weinberger construction; Mio and Ranicki on the quadratic form E8 and exotic homology manifolds; Pedersen and Yamasaki on stability in controlled L-theory; Quinn on open problems; and Ranicki–Yamasaki on controlled L-theory. It is useful as a snapshot of how geometric decomposition theory, controlled topology, and L-theory fit together around generalized manifolds.

## 3. Noncommutative localization in algebra and topology

- **Editor:** Andrew Ranicki
- **Conference:** ICMS Edinburgh, 29–30 April 2002
- **Published:** London Mathematical Society Lecture Note Series 330, Cambridge University Press, 2006
- **Length:** 323 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/nlat.pdf

A proceedings volume organized around the idea of **localization when multiplication does not commute**. In ordinary commutative algebra one localizes a ring by formally making selected elements invertible; in the noncommutative setting this becomes subtler and connects to Ore localization, Cohn/universal localization, derived categories, group rings, K-theory and topology.

The contents make the breadth unusually clear: J. A. Beachy on flatness and the Ore condition; P. M. Cohn on the history of localization in general rings; W. G. Dwyer on homotopy theory; P. A. Linnell on group rings; A. Neeman on a noncommutative form of Thomason localization; Ranicki on topological applications; H. Reich on L²-Betti numbers and isomorphism conjectures; Desmond Sheiham on boundary-link cobordism and Blanchfield–Duval forms; and Z. Škoda on noncommutative geometry. The published volume is also dedicated to Desmond Sheiham.

## 4. Geometric Topology: Localization, Periodicity and Galois Symmetry

- **Author:** Dennis Sullivan
- **Editor:** Andrew Ranicki
- **Original notes:** MIT, 1970
- **Edited publication:** 2005
- **Length:** 296 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/gtop.pdf

These are Dennis Sullivan's influential 1970 MIT notes, edited and republished by Ranicki. The main subjects are localization and completion in homotopy theory, spherical fibrations, algebraic-geometric ideas in topology, and a striking "Galois group" viewpoint on geometric topology.

Ranicki's editor's preface emphasizes that these notes helped establish p-local, profinite and rational methods in homotopy theory and fed into the solution of the Adams conjecture relating vector bundles and spherical fibrations. The volume includes the original notes, Sullivan's related 1970 ICM article on Galois symmetry in manifold theory, and a 2004 postscript. This is primarily Sullivan's mathematics; Ranicki's role here is preservation, editing and contextualization.

## 5. Algebraic and Geometric Surgery

- **Author:** Andrew Ranicki
- **Published:** Oxford Mathematical Monograph, 2002
- **Electronic version:** corrected/expanded, 2014
- **Length:** 374 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/surgery.pdf

A broad treatment of surgery theory that deliberately develops the **geometry and the algebra together**. The early chapters build the geometric machinery: manifolds, Morse theory, handles, homotopy and homology, Poincaré duality, bundles, cobordism, embeddings, immersions, singularities, Whitehead torsion, and Poincaré complexes.

The later chapters turn this into surgery machinery for maps: normal maps, surgery below the middle dimension, quadratic forms and formations, even- and odd-dimensional L-groups, and the corresponding surgery obstructions. The electronic text incorporates errata from the printed edition plus later corrections and comments. Compared with Ranicki's more specialized L-theory books, this is the most direct single route from the geometric operation called surgery to the algebraic obstruction groups that decide when it can be completed.

## 6. Quadratic Forms and Their Applications

- **Editors:** Eva Bayer-Fluckiger, David Lewis, Andrew Ranicki
- **Conference:** University College Dublin, 5–9 July 1999
- **Published:** Contemporary Mathematics 272, AMS, 2000
- **Length:** 314 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/dublin.pdf

Proceedings of a large conference on quadratic forms, deliberately ranging far beyond one subfield. The preface says the meeting covered applications in algebra, number theory, algebraic geometry, topology and information theory.

The table of contents is unusually rich historically and mathematically: Bayer-Fluckiger on Galois cohomology of classical groups; J. H. Conway on universal quadratic forms and the fifteen theorem; Manjul Bhargava on the Conway–Schneeberger fifteen theorem; Kearton on quadratic forms in knot theory; Ina Kersten's biography of Ernst Witt; Scharlau on the history of the algebraic theory of quadratic forms; work on splitting, hermitian forms and local densities; constructive approaches to Hilbert's ternary-quartic theorem; and C. T. C. Wall on quadratic forms and normal surface singularities. This is a survey/research collection rather than a single linear textbook.

## 7. Surgery on Compact Manifolds

- **Author:** C. T. C. Wall
- **Second-edition editor:** Andrew Ranicki
- **Second edition:** 1999
- **Length:** 314 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/scm.pdf

Wall's classic foundational book on high-dimensional surgery, with Ranicki editing the second edition. The text develops surgery below the middle dimension, simple Poincaré complexes and then the main surgery theorem in even and odd dimensions, including bounded cases.

The second half is heavily application-oriented: manifold structures on Poincaré complexes, submanifolds, explicit surgery obstruction groups and obstruction calculations, and applications such as free group actions on spheres. The book is historically central because Wall's L-groups are the original home of the algebraic obstructions that Ranicki later reformulated using algebraic Poincaré complexes. Credit here should remain primarily **C. T. C. Wall, author**, with Ranicki credited as editor of the second edition.

## 8. High-dimensional knot theory: Algebraic surgery in codimension 2

- **Author:** Andrew Ranicki
- **Published:** Springer Mathematical Monograph, 1998
- **Length:** 691 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/knot.pdf

A very large book applying algebraic surgery to knots in codimension two. Ranicki starts from the general idea of an n-knot as an embedding of an n-sphere in an (n+2)-sphere, so ordinary knots are only the n=1 case.

The book builds a large algebraic toolkit around polynomial and Laurent extensions, algebraic K-theory localization, Novikov homology, noncommutative localization, endomorphism K-theory, codimension-q surgery, Seifert surfaces, Blanchfield complexes, several forms of L-theory, signatures and multisignatures, and finally knot cobordism groups. It explicitly connects classical Seifert-matrix/Blanchfield-type data to higher-dimensional surgery theory. The preface also gives a useful personal history: Ranicki describes beginning graduate work under Frank Adams, reading Novikov under Andrew Casson's actual supervision, and developing the material that became his early algebraic L-theory work.

## 9. Surgery and Geometric Topology

- **Editors:** Andrew Ranicki and Masayuki Yamasaki
- **Conference:** Josai University, Sakado, September 1996
- **Length:** 162 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/josai.pdf

Conference proceedings covering a broad slice of late-1990s surgery and geometric topology. The collection includes work on Coxeter groups, stratified ends, braid structures in mapping class groups, controlled topology, the Novikov conjecture, group actions on spheres, knots and links in all dimensions, low-dimensional topology, chain duality, controlled L-theory, surgery and C*-algebras, and infinite-dimensional manifold tuples.

Contributors include Toshiyuki Akita, Frank Connolly, Bogdan Vajiac, Zbigniew Fiedorowicz, Yongjin Song, Bruce Hughes, Tsuyoshi Kato, Tomohiro Kawakami, Masaharu Morimoto, Eiji Ogasa, Erik Pedersen, Frank Quinn, Andrew Ranicki, Masayuki Yamasaki, John Roe and Tatsuhiko Yagasaki. Ranicki's own contribution includes slides on chain duality, and he coauthored the controlled-L-theory announcement with Yamasaki.

## 10. The Hauptvermutung Book

- **Editor:** Andrew Ranicki
- **Contributors:** A. J. Casson, D. P. Sullivan, M. A. Armstrong, C. P. Rourke, G. E. Cooke, and Ranicki
- **Published:** 1996
- **Length:** 194 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/haupt.pdf

A historical/documentary collection about the **Hauptvermutung**, the once-central conjecture that different triangulations of the same topological space should admit a common subdivision. In manifold topology the failure and repair of variants of this conjecture became entangled with PL structures, smoothing, surgery, characteristic classes and the Rochlin invariant.

Ranicki opens with a 1996 survey covering the polyhedral and manifold Hauptvermutung and homology manifolds. The volume then republishes A. J. Casson's 1967 notes on block bundles, Dennis Sullivan's 1967 seminar notes on triangulating homotopy equivalences and homeomorphisms, and the Princeton notes by Armstrong, Rourke and Cooke describing the Hauptvermutung through the work of Lashof–Rothenberg and Casson–Sullivan. It is valuable as much as an archive of historically important notes as a conventional monograph.

## 11. Ends of Complexes

- **Authors:** Bruce Hughes and Andrew Ranicki
- **Published:** Cambridge Tracts in Mathematics 123, 1996
- **Length:** 374 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/ends.pdf

A systematic book about what happens **at infinity** in noncompact topology and its algebraic analogues. The authors intentionally use "complex" in both senses: CW/simplicial complexes and chain complexes. An end is treated as a form of infinite behavior — noncompactness geometrically and infinite generation algebraically.

Part I develops topology at infinity: end spaces, homology and homotopy at infinity, tameness, projective class and infinite torsion. Part II studies topology over the real line using infinite cyclic covers, mapping tori, ribbons, bands, approximate fibrations and wrapping/relaxation constructions. Part III gives the algebraic versions using polynomial extensions, algebraic tameness and K- and L-theory. The introduction explicitly places this theory in the classification of high-dimensional manifolds, the Hauptvermutung, Kirby–Siebenmann theory, Whitehead torsion and Novikov-type problems.

## 12. Algebraic L-theory and Topological Manifolds

- **Author:** Andrew Ranicki
- **Original publication:** Cambridge Tracts in Mathematics 102, 1992
- **PDF version:** corrected and expanded; dated January 2018
- **Length:** 365 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/topman.pdf

One of Ranicki's core books on turning manifold surgery into an algebraic theory. It develops algebraic Poincaré complexes, algebraic normal complexes, bordism categories, duality and assembly, then constructs algebraic L-spectra and the algebraic surgery exact sequence.

The later half reconnects the algebra to topology: L-theory orientations, the total surgery obstruction, structure sets, geometric Poincaré complexes, transfer, finite fundamental groups, splitting, higher signatures, four-periodic L-theory and surgery with coefficients. The central theme is that the question "is this Poincaré-like space actually homotopy equivalent to a manifold?" can be encoded in algebraic L-theory, with the **total surgery obstruction** serving as the decisive obstruction in the high-dimensional setting.

## 13. Lower K- and L-theory

- **Author:** Andrew Ranicki
- **Original publication:** London Mathematical Society Lecture Note Series 178, 1992
- **Electronic reprint:** 2001, corrected/expanded
- **Length:** 177 pages
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/lowerkl.pdf

A compact but technical book about the negative/lower parts of algebraic K- and L-theory and how they arise in topology. Ranicki frames K-groups and L-groups as obstruction groups for geometric structures: Whitehead torsion and finiteness on the K-theory side, and surgery obstructions on the L-theory side.

The chapters cover projective class and torsion, graded/bounded categories, end invariants, excision and transversality, open cones, Laurent polynomial extensions, nilpotent classes, lower K-theory, quadratic and symmetric L-theory, lower L-theory, transfers and algebraic fibering obstructions. A recurring idea is that crossing with tori corresponds algebraically to iterated Laurent polynomial extensions, and lower groups measure what obstructs stabilizing a space into the desired finite/manifold form.

## 14. Exact Sequences in the Algebraic Theory of Surgery

- **Author:** Andrew Ranicki
- **Published:** Princeton Mathematical Notes 26, 1981
- **Length:** about 864 pages; the searchable PDF is very large
- **PDF:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/books/exacsrch.pdf

An early foundational monument of Ranicki's algebraic surgery program. The book develops the exact-sequence machinery needed to make algebraic L-theory behave as a practical obstruction theory in the same way that exact sequences organize algebraic K-theory.

The introductory material connects the theory to Wall's surgery obstruction and assumes the classical K-theoretic background of projective class, finiteness obstruction and Whitehead torsion. Sections 2–6 develop algebraic L-theory exact sequences, including change-of-rings/localization phenomena; the later material develops an algebraic theory of codimension-q surgery. The larger conceptual move is to replace manifold surgery problems by surgery on chain complexes carrying algebraic Poincaré duality, so geometric obstructions can be calculated and transported through exact sequences.

## Related book-index item: Algebraic and geometric topology — Rutgers 1983

- **Editors:** Andrew Ranicki, Norman Levitt and Frank Quinn
- **Published:** Springer Lecture Notes in Mathematics 1126, 1985
- **Index page:** https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/surgery/rutgers/index.htm

The Ranicki book index does not point to a single full-volume PDF here; it points to a page containing the whole volume as DJVU plus individually linked papers. The proceedings include Assadi–Vogel on semifree finite-group actions, Cappell–Shaneson on torsion in L-groups, Jim Davis on higher diagonals and K(π,1)s, Jerry Levine on homotopy spheres, Levitt on local formulae for p₁, Milgram on Swan finiteness and the Cappell–Shaneson example, Pedersen–Weibel on nonconnective algebraic K-theory, Frank Quinn on geometric algebra, Ranicki on algebraic torsion, Justin Smith on equivariant Moore spaces, Vogell on the involution in K-theory of spaces, Waldhausen on algebraic K-theory of spaces, and short papers by Sondow and Weinberger.

I am keeping it in this index because it is clearly part of Ranicki's posted book archive, but it is a collection of component documents rather than one hosted PDF like the fourteen entries above.
