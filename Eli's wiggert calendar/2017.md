# Complex Beauties 2017 — lesson seeds

Primary source: [official *Complex Beauties* archive](https://blogs.hrz.tu-freiberg.de/mathekalender/english/) and the author-uploaded 2017 calendar. Calendar assets remain external.

| Month | Function / pictured object | Person / historical story | Reading and citation trail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | **Complex powers** `z^a = exp(a log z)` and branch behavior. | **M. C. Escher.** Escher's repeated transformations and logarithmic spirals make a useful visual bridge into the multivalued logarithm without pretending that he invented the mathematics. | [Exponentiation of complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponentiation#Complex_exponents) · de Smit & Lenstra, “Artful Mathematics: The Heritage of M. C. Escher,” *Notices AMS* 50(4) (2003). |
| February | **Julia/Fatou dynamics.** Guest contribution by Walter Bergweiler. | **Adrien Douady.** Douady helped build the modern rigorous theory of polynomial dynamics and made the Mandelbrot set a central mathematical object. | [Adrien Douady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrien_Douady) · [Complex dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_dynamics). |
| March | **Padé approximation**, including an approximant to `tan(z^3)` and Froissart zero-pole pairs. Guest contribution by Stefan Güttel. | **Henri Padé.** Matching a power series with a rational function often continues a function far beyond the reach of Taylor polynomials—but can also create spurious near-cancelling poles and zeros. | [Padé approximant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pad%C3%A9_approximant) · Baker & Graves-Morris, *Padé Approximants*. |
| April | **Quasiconformal maps and the Grötzsch inequality.** | **Herbert Grötzsch.** Measuring controlled angle distortion led to the extremal theory that became quasiconformal mapping. | [Quasiconformal mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiconformal_mapping) · Ahlfors, *Lectures on Quasiconformal Mappings* (1958). |
| May | **Complex extension of the Collatz map**, calendar formula `f(x)=1/4(2+7x-(2+5x)cos πx)` and an iterate. | **Lothar Collatz.** An elementary integer iteration can be embedded into an analytic function, letting complex dynamics ask what the discrete conjecture looks like off the integers. | [Collatz conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collatz_conjecture) · literature on analytic extensions of the 3x+1 map. |
| June | **Weierstrass sigma function**, with `g(z)=e^{az}σ(z)` arranged to have doubly periodic phase. | **Karl Weierstrass.** Sigma encodes a lattice of zeros; exponential correction can change the function while preserving striking periodic phase geometry. | [Weierstrass sigma function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_functions) · Semmler & Wegert, “Meromorphic Functions with Doubly Periodic Phase,” *Comput. Methods Funct. Theory*. |
| July | **Barnes G-function**, `G(z+1)=Γ(z)G(z)`. | **Ernest William Barnes.** If gamma interpolates factorials, Barnes G interpolates products of factorials; it is the first step into multiple-gamma hierarchies. | [Barnes G-function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnes_G-function) · V. S. Adamchik, ISSAC 2001 work on the Barnes function. |
| August | **Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem**, visualizing the analytic PDE solution at a later time. | **Sofya Kovalevskaya.** The theorem guarantees local analytic solutions for a broad class of analytic PDE initial-value problems; it also carries a layered Cauchy/Kovalevskaya history. | [Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy%E2%80%93Kovalevskaya_theorem) · [Kovalevskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofya_Kovalevskaya). |
| September | **Complex Ginzburg–Landau equation and pattern formation.** | **Vitaly Ginzburg.** A phenomenological superconductivity model became a universal amplitude equation for oscillations, defects and spatiotemporal chaos. | [Complex Ginzburg–Landau equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_Ginzburg%E2%80%93Landau_equation) · Aranson & Kramer, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* 74 (2002), 99. |
| October | **Riccati differential equation.** | **Jacopo Riccati.** A nonlinear first-order ODE can be converted to a second-order linear equation; complex continuation then reveals poles and movable singularities. | [Riccati equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riccati_equation) · [Jacopo Riccati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_Riccati). |
| November | **Lorenz equations**, emphasizing complex-time singularities rather than only the real butterfly attractor. | **Edward Lorenz.** Deterministic weather equations produced sensitive dependence; complex singularities help explain limits on analytic continuation of the trajectories. | [Lorenz system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenz_system) · Viswanath & Şahutoğlu, *SIAM J. Sci. Comput.* (2012), plus work by Webb & Trefethen. |
| December | **Theta functions and the partition generating function / circle method.** | **G. H. Hardy.** Hardy and Ramanujan used complex analysis around the unit circle to extract asymptotics for partition numbers. | [Hardy–Ramanujan asymptotic formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_(number_theory)#Asymptotic_formulas) · Hardy & Ramanujan (1918). |

## Video directions

March can visibly contrast Taylor and Padé continuation; May can mark the integers first and then reveal the analytic Collatz extension between them; June is a natural precursor to the torus/doubly-periodic animation; November should begin with the familiar Lorenz butterfly and then ask where its trajectory stops being analytic in complex time.
