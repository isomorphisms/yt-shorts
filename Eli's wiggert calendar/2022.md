# Complex Beauties 2022 — lesson seeds

Primary source: Wegert, Semmler, Gorkin & Daepp, *Complex Beauties 2022*, author-uploaded full text; [official archive](https://blogs.hrz.tu-freiberg.de/mathekalender/english/). Calendar assets remain external.

| Month | Function / pictured object | Person / historical story | Reading and citation trail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | **Bernstein basis polynomial** `b_{13,9}(z)=C(13,9)z^9(1-z)^4` and Bernstein approximation. | **Sergei Bernstein.** His constructive proof of Weierstrass approximation replaces existence with an explicit averaging formula; the same basis later became fundamental in Bézier curves. | [Bernstein polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernstein_polynomial) · [Bézier curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9zier_curve). |
| February | **Harmonic polynomial** with many zeros, a modified Wilmshurst-type example of degree seven. | **Peter Duren.** Duren's work on univalent and harmonic mappings helped make planar harmonic functions a geometric function theory in their own right. | [Harmonic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_function) · Duren, *Harmonic Mappings in the Plane*; Duren, Herbig & Khavinson on zeros of harmonic polynomials. |
| March | **Jentzsch theorem**, shown with the twentieth partial sum of `Σ k^2 z^{k(k-1)/2}` and zeros accumulating near its circle of convergence. | **Robert Jentzsch.** Partial sums of a power series know where analytic continuation fails: their zeros crowd every point of the circle of convergence. | [Jentzsch theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jentzsch_theorem) · Duren, Herbig & Khavinson, *Math. Intelligencer* 30(3) (2008). |
| April | **Sochocki–Plemelj jump relation**, with `F(z)=-(1/(2πi))log((z+1)/(z-1))`. | **Julian Sochocki.** Sochocki published the boundary-jump formula in 1868; Plemelj later developed the theory in the context of singular integral equations. | [Sokhotski–Plemelj theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokhotski%E2%80%93Plemelj_theorem) · Sochocki (1868) and Plemelj (1908). |
| May | **Weierstrass's nowhere-differentiable function (“monster”).** | **Grace Chisholm Young.** Young studied infinite derivatives and real-function pathology; Hardy later relaxed Weierstrass's parameter restrictions. The story is how nineteenth-century analysis learned that continuity does not imply smoothness. | [Weierstrass function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_function) · Hardy (1916) on Weierstrass's nondifferentiable function · [Grace Chisholm Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Chisholm_Young). |
| June | **Kantorovich theorem for Newton iteration**, extended in the calendar to harmonic maps through Wirtinger derivatives; picture involves `N^3(z)-z`. Guest contribution by Olivier Sète and Jan Zur. | **Leonid Kantorovich.** Newton's method becomes a theorem with quantitative hypotheses in Banach spaces: not just “iterate and hope,” but bounds guaranteeing existence and convergence. | [Kantorovich theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantorovich_theorem) · Kantorovich's 1948 Newton-method paper. |
| July | **Caustics of a harmonic map**, using `f(z)=1/2 φ(z)^2+φ(z)-η` with `φ(z)=exp(2z^3+1)`. Guest contribution by Sète and Zur. | **Vladimir Arnold.** Singularity/catastrophe theory classifies the stable shapes that appear when projections fold, including the same fold/cusp geometry seen in optical caustics. | [Caustic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caustic_(optics)) · [Catastrophe theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catastrophe_theory) · Arnold's work on singularities of differentiable maps. |
| August | **Linear multistep method stability**, plotting the error/stability function `ρ(z)/σ(z)-log z` for an implicit four-step Adams–Moulton method. | **Germund Dahlquist.** Dahlquist turned numerical ODE methods into a stability theory and proved barriers on what linear multistep schemes can achieve. | [Linear multistep method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_multistep_method) · [Dahlquist barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahlquist_barrier) · Dahlquist, *Math. Scand.* 4 (1956), 33–53. |
| September | **Korteweg–de Vries equation** `u_t+6uu_x+u_{xxx}=0` and traveling-wave/soliton behavior. | **Diederik Korteweg.** The standard name credits Korteweg and de Vries (1895), but Boussinesq had already derived the relevant long-wave equation in the 1870s; the attribution itself is worth preserving. | [Korteweg–De Vries equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korteweg%E2%80%93De_Vries_equation) · Willink, arXiv:0710.5227 on the history. |
| October | **Nonlinear Schrödinger equation** `iu_t+u_{xx}-2σu|u|^2=0`, with a three-soliton solution. Guest contribution by Andrei Bogatyrev. | **Alexei Shabat.** Zakharov and Shabat's inverse-scattering method made NLS one of the canonical integrable systems: nonlinear evolution solved through a spectral problem. | [Nonlinear Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_Schr%C3%B6dinger_equation) · Zakharov & Shabat (1972) on exact theory/inverse scattering. |
| November | **Dessins d'enfants / Belyi maps**, with triangulations and conformal/circle-packing viewpoints. | **Alexander Grothendieck.** Grothendieck's *Esquisse d'un Programme* reframed simple embedded graphs as a way to study the absolute Galois group; this revisits the March 2016 Belyi entry from the arithmetic/combinatorial side. | [Dessins d'enfants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dessin_d%27enfant) · Grothendieck, *Esquisse d'un Programme* · Bowers & Stephenson on circle packing/conformal tilings. |
| December | **Asymptotic self-similarity of Mandelbrot and Julia sets.** | **Tan Lei.** Tan proved local similarity results explaining why zooms near certain Mandelbrot parameters reproduce related Julia-set geometry—not exact global self-similarity, but a precise asymptotic statement. | [Tan Lei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan_Lei) · Tan Lei, *Commun. Math. Phys.* 134 (1990), 587–617. |
