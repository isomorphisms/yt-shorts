# Complex Beauties 2012 — source-audit lesson seeds

Primary source: [TU Freiberg's official *Complex Beauties* archive](https://blogs.hrz.tu-freiberg.de/mathekalender/english/). The calendar PDF and artwork remain external. The rows below record the pictured construction in words and formulas so it can be independently re-rendered for video; page links are provenance, not vendored assets.

| Month | Function / pictured object | Person / historical story | Reading and citation trail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | **CRC-16-CCITT generator polynomial** `f(z)=1+z^5+z^12+z^16`, shown on roughly `|Re z|,|Im z|≤1.4`. The visual is the complex phase portrait of the same four-term coefficient pattern used over `GF(2)` for cyclic redundancy checks. | **William Wesley Peterson.** Peterson and Daniel T. Brown's 1961 paper made polynomial division over the two-element field into a practical error-detection scheme. | [Calendar pp. 3–4](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/sdwrddb9iii9z09guqplr/cal12.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=gak31764paul4rj5vq8nybme1#page=4) · Peterson & Brown, “Cyclic Codes for Error Detection” (1961), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/JRPROC.1961.287814). |
| February | **`(z^18-z)/(z-1)=z+z^2+...+z^17` viewed around infinity on the Riemann sphere.** The calendar changes stereographic chart so `z=∞` appears at the center; the finite zeros lie on the unit circle while infinity is a pole of order 17. | **Claudius Ptolemy.** The page uses the history of stereographic projection, naming Ptolemy's *Planisphaerium*, then connects the projection to Riemann's extended complex plane. | [Calendar pp. 5–6](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/sdwrddb9iii9z09guqplr/cal12.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=gak31764paul4rj5vq8nybme1#page=6) · [Stereographic projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereographic_projection) · [Riemann sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_sphere). |
| March | **Finite Blaschke products.** Zeros in the unit disk generate rational inner functions whose boundary values have modulus one. | **Wilhelm Blaschke.** Blaschke products are the disk analogue of building an analytic function directly from its zeros. | [Blaschke product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaschke_product) · [Wilhelm Blaschke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Blaschke). |
| April | **60th Taylor section of the exponential**, `P_60(z)=Σ_{k=0}^{60} z^k/k!`, with zeros approaching the **Szegő curve** after scaling. This is a good animation: increase the truncation degree and watch the zero cloud organize around the limiting curve. **Source caution:** the calendar text prints `P_n(z/n)`, while the standard zero scaling is `P_n(nz)`; do not silently erase that discrepancy in a reproduction. | **Gábor Szegő.** His work on orthogonal polynomials and asymptotic zero distributions gives the limiting curve behind the picture. | [Calendar pp. 9–10](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/sdwrddb9iii9z09guqplr/cal12.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=gak31764paul4rj5vq8nybme1#page=10) · Szegő, *Orthogonal Polynomials* · Pólya & Szegő, *Problems and Theorems in Analysis*. |
| May | **Complex tangent** `tan z=sin z/cos z`, with zeros at `kπ`, poles at `π/2+kπ`, and rapid convergence toward `i` or `-i` high in the upper/lower half-plane. A useful render is a vertical pan showing the repeating zero-pole strip and the nearly constant far-field phase. | **Thomas Fincke.** The page credits Fincke's 1583 *Geometriae rotundi libri XIIII* with introducing the term “tangent,” while crediting Abu al-Wafa with an earlier definition. | [Calendar pp. 11–12](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/sdwrddb9iii9z09guqplr/cal12.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=gak31764paul4rj5vq8nybme1#page=12) · [Complex trigonometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_functions#Complex_arguments). |
| June | **Gaussian 0.1-values:** `g(z)=exp(-z^2)-0.1`, on roughly `|Re z|,|Im z|≤5`. The zeros solve `exp(-z^2)=0.1` and appear along two hyperbola-like branches. Animate the constant `0.1` to show how `a`-values move. | **Carl Friedrich Gauss.** The calendar uses the Gaussian/error-curve connection and then turns the real bell curve into a complex zero-set problem. | [Calendar pp. 13–14](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/sdwrddb9iii9z09guqplr/cal12.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=gak31764paul4rj5vq8nybme1#page=14) · the calendar refers back to January 2011 and to Gauss's *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae*. |
| July | **Julia sets / polynomial iteration.** The 2013 calendar explicitly points back to the July 2012 complex-dynamics entry. | **Gaston Julia.** Julia and Fatou developed the early iteration theory that later fed directly into Mandelbrot-set dynamics. | [Julia set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_set) · [Gaston Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Julia) · Milnor, *Dynamics in One Complex Variable*. |
| August | **Gamma function `Γ(z)`.** The calendar series later points back to the August 2012 gamma-function picture. | Historical line: **Euler → Legendre → Weierstrass**. Euler's integral and product viewpoints become a meromorphic continuation with poles at the non-positive integers. | [Gamma function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_function) · [Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonhard_Euler) · Whittaker & Watson, *A Course of Modern Analysis*. |
| September | **Truncated Lambert series** `F_10(z)=Σ_{k=1}^{10} z^k/(1-z^k)`. The infinite series satisfies `Σ_{k≥1} z^k/(1-z^k)=Σ_{n≥1} d(n)z^n` for `|z|<1`, so the complex picture links poles at roots of unity to the divisor-counting generating function. | **Johann Heinrich Lambert.** The page uses generating functions and Lambert's name as the route from a rational-looking sum to arithmetic coefficients. | [Calendar pp. 19–20](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/sdwrddb9iii9z09guqplr/cal12.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=gak31764paul4rj5vq8nybme1#page=20) · [Lambert series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambert_series). The calendar does not identify an original Lambert publication for this formula. |
| October | **Bessel function `J_n` from the wave equation.** The 2013 Neumann-function entry explicitly says October 2012 treated the wave equation in polar coordinates and used `J_n`. | **Friedrich Bessel.** Separation of variables turns circular membrane modes into Bessel's differential equation. | [Bessel function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel_function) · [Bessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Bessel) · NIST DLMF, Chapter 10. |
| November | **Nonlinear first-order differential equation `y' = y^2 + x^2`**, treated through a substitution in the Bernoulli/Riccati tradition. | **Jakob Bernoulli.** The useful story is the seventeenth-century search for substitutions that turn nonlinear differential equations into tractable forms. | [Riccati equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riccati_equation) · [Bernoulli differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_differential_equation) · [Jacob Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Bernoulli). |
| December | **Klein modular invariant** `j(z)=12^3 g_2(z)^3/(g_2(z)^3-27g_3(z)^2)` on the upper half-plane, with `g_2,g_3` given by lattice sums. The picture exposes the symmetries `z→z+1` and `z→-1/z`; a clean video can render a fundamental region and then tile it by those transformations. | **Felix Klein.** The page connects the modular function to Klein's geometry and rivalry with Poincaré over automorphic functions. | [Calendar pp. 25–26](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/sdwrddb9iii9z09guqplr/cal12.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=gak31764paul4rj5vq8nybme1#page=26) · [Klein j-invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J-invariant) · Klein's 1872 Erlangen Program is named by the calendar. |

## Source-audit result

The seven previously unresolved months are now identified from the calendar-level audit rather than inferred from the featured names. The calendar PDF and artwork are still external. These notes describe the mathematics closely enough to make original phase/domain-coloring renders, with source-page links retained for attribution and checking.

## Video directions

Good early experiments: build the January CRC polynomial from its four binary terms; move February's view through infinity by switching stereographic chart; grow April's exponential sections while plotting their zeros; pan through May's tangent zero-pole lattice; vary June's target `a`; grow September's Lambert truncation; and tile December's modular picture by `z→z+1` and `z→-1/z`.
