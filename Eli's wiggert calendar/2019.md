# Complex Beauties 2019 — lesson seeds

Primary source: [official *Complex Beauties* archive](https://blogs.hrz.tu-freiberg.de/mathekalender/english/) and the author-uploaded 2019 calendar. Calendar assets remain external.

| Month | Function / pictured object | Person / historical story | Reading and citation trail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | **Faber polynomials**, with the calendar showing the degree-36 polynomial for a square. | **Georg Faber.** A conformal map of the exterior of a compact set generates polynomials adapted to that shape; the square makes the contrast with ordinary monomials visible. | [Faber polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faber_polynomials) · J. H. Curtiss, “Faber Polynomials and the Faber Series,” *Amer. Math. Monthly* 78(6) (1971). |
| February | **Finite differences**, including Newton's transformation of the arctangent series. | **Isaac Newton.** Before modern calculus notation, tables of differences were both an interpolation tool and a way to transform slowly convergent series. | [Finite difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_difference) · Ranjan Roy, historical work on Newton's series and interpolation. |
| March | **Lagrange remainder** in Taylor's theorem, complexified. | **Joseph-Louis Lagrange.** The familiar real error bound hides an analytic remainder function whose zeros, growth and geometry can be plotted directly. | [Taylor's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor%27s_theorem) · [Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph-Louis_Lagrange). |
| April | **Magnetic vortices / 2D skyrmion complex representation.** Guest contribution by Andrei Bogatyrev and Konstantin Metlov. | **William Thomson (Lord Kelvin).** Kelvin's vortex ideas provide the historical hook; the modern object is a magnetic texture efficiently encoded by complex functions. | [Magnetic skyrmion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_skyrmion) · Bogatyrev & Metlov, arXiv:1711.07317. |
| May | **Fejér–Jackson–Grönwall inequality / Gibbs phenomenon.** | **Thomas Hakon Grönwall.** Grönwall's 1912 paper studied overshoot near Fourier-series jumps; this month pairs naturally with the later 2020 Gibbs entry. | [Gibbs phenomenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbs_phenomenon) · Grönwall, “Über die Gibbssche Erscheinung...” *Math. Ann.* (1912). |
| June | **Singular inner function** generated by a nonatomic Cantor measure. Guest contribution by Sheldon Axler. | **Donald Sarason.** Inner functions can have boundary modulus one almost everywhere without being finite Blaschke products; singular measures supply the missing factor. | [Inner function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_function) · Sarason, *Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.* 127 (1967), 179–203. |
| July | **Corona theorem**, illustrated through an infinite Blaschke product with pathological boundary behavior. | **Thomas H. Wolff.** Carleson solved the corona problem; Wolff's work gave major refinements and alternative ideas in analytic function spaces. | [Corona theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corona_theorem) · overview in *Notices AMS* 48(5) (2001). |
| August | **Analytic functions with prescribed boundary values.** | **Paul Halmos.** The calendar connects a boundary-value construction to operator/function theory; the visual question is how much interior analytic behavior boundary data can force. | [Hardy space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy_space) · [Paul Halmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Halmos). |
| September | **Luzin–Privalov uniqueness theorem** for boundary values of analytic functions. | **Nikolai Luzin.** A function that has boundary limit zero on a set of positive measure is forced to vanish identically under the theorem's hypotheses. Luzin's later political persecution is a separate historical story worth handling carefully. | [Nikolai Luzin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Luzin) · [Luzin affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzin_affair) · literature on Privalov boundary uniqueness. |
| October | **BMO (bounded mean oscillation)** and its analytic-function connections. | **Cora Sadosky.** BMO sits at a critical endpoint of harmonic analysis; Sadosky's career also connects the theory to operator methods and Hilbert transforms. | [Bounded mean oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_mean_oscillation) · memorial chapter on Sadosky, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-30961-3_3. |
| November | **Fejér kernel**, complexified: `F_n(z)=1/n (sin(nz/2)/sin(z/2))^2`; calendar front uses `F_5`. | **Lipót Fejér.** Cesàro averaging turns badly behaved Fourier partial sums into a positive approximation kernel and suppresses the worst Gibbs oscillation. | [Fejér kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fej%C3%A9r_kernel) · [Fejér's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fej%C3%A9r%27s_theorem). |
| December | **Siegel disks** around irrationally indifferent fixed points. | **Carl Ludwig Siegel.** An orbit can rotate forever without converging or becoming chaotic; arithmetic conditions on the rotation number decide whether the dynamics linearizes. | [Siegel disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegel_disc) · [Siegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Ludwig_Siegel). |

## Video directions

January can morph the boundary from circle to square while the adapted Faber polynomial changes; May/November make a natural Gibbs-versus-Fejér pair; June can build an inner function from a Cantor measure; December should zoom into an indifferent fixed point and contrast a Siegel disk with nearby chaotic Julia-set behavior.
