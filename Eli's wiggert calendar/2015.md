# Complex Beauties 2015 — source-audit lesson seeds

Primary source: Wegert, Semmler, Gorkin & Daepp, *Complex Beauties 2015*, DOI [10.13140/2.1.3025.7442](https://doi.org/10.13140/2.1.3025.7442), plus the [official archive](https://blogs.hrz.tu-freiberg.de/mathekalender/english/). The calendar PDF and artwork remain external. These notes describe the pictured constructions closely enough for independent video renders and call out any data the calendar itself omits.

| Month | Function / lesson direction | Person / historical story | Reading and citation trail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | **Wilkinson polynomial / sensitivity of polynomial roots.** | **James Hardy Wilkinson.** Tiny coefficient perturbations in a perfectly explicit polynomial can move roots dramatically: a foundational lesson in numerical conditioning. | [Wilkinson's polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilkinson%27s_polynomial) · Wilkinson, *Rounding Errors in Algebraic Processes* (1963). |
| February | **Prescribed boundary colors from elementary geometry.** For a unit-circle arc from `a_k` to `b_k`, use `f_k(z)=c_k((z-a_k)/(z-b_k))^(α_k)` with a branch analytic in the disk, then multiply the factors. Constant phase of `(z-a)/(z-b)` gives circular arcs; constant modulus gives Apollonian circles. The calendar radially extends the boundary coloring outside the disk. A video can build the boundary pattern one factor at a time. | **Euclid.** The construction turns the inscribed-angle theorem into complex argument geometry; Apollonian distance-ratio circles supply the complementary modulus picture. | [Calendar pp. 5–6](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/i6b417bdqvnz37uq9du89/cal15.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=llt20vyjqiazl7zk130ogorfj#page=6) · Euclid, *Elements*, [Project Gutenberg edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/21076) · [Apollonian circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circles_of_Apollonius). |
| March | **Hilbert-operator reconstruction from boundary phase.** On the unit circle, `H v(e^{iτ})=(1/(2π))∫ v(e^{iσ}) cot((σ-τ)/2)dσ`; given phase `φ`, set boundary values `f=exp(Hφ+iφ)` and analytically extend them into the disk. Numerically the cotangent singularity needs a principal-value or Fourier implementation. A natural animation starts with a phase function on the circle and fills in the compatible interior magnitude. | **David Hilbert.** The page places Hilbert's singular-integral machinery in Riemann's program of determining analytic functions from boundary data. | [Calendar pp. 7–8](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/i6b417bdqvnz37uq9du89/cal15.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=llt20vyjqiazl7zk130ogorfj#page=8) · Riemann's 1851 thesis, [GDZ scan](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/id/DE-611-HS-3206233) · Hilbert's 1904 integral-equation work, [EuDML record](https://eudml.org/doc/58572). |
| April | **Riemann–Hilbert problem with index 7.** Seek analytic `f` in the disk with boundary condition `Im(conj(ψ)f)=c`. The pictured case uses `ψ(t)=t^7`, `c=0`, and a solution chosen to have seven zeros in the first quadrant. The winding number of `ψ` controls the solution-space dimension; this is the topological ingredient missing from a purely local integral-equation view. | **Fritz Noether.** His 1921 paper made the index/winding-number obstruction explicit and is an important ancestor of modern Fredholm/operator index theory. | [Calendar pp. 9–10](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/i6b417bdqvnz37uq9du89/cal15.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=llt20vyjqiazl7zk130ogorfj#page=10) · Noether, “Über eine Klasse singulärer Integralgleichungen” (1921), [EuDML](https://eudml.org/doc/158833), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01457974). |
| May | **Wiener–Hopf factorization.** Find `Φ_+` analytic inside the unit circle and `Φ_-` outside so `Φ_+(t)=G(t)Φ_-(t)+g(t)` on the boundary. In the homogeneous index-zero case, factor `G=Φ_+/Φ_-` by splitting logarithmic/Fourier data into inside- and outside-analytic pieces. The calendar chooses `G` real and negative around the whole circle, so the two phase portraits meet in complementary boundary colors. | **Norbert Wiener.** Wiener and Eberhard Hopf developed the factorization for a stellar radiative-equilibrium integral equation, joining complex boundary problems to Fourier methods. | [Calendar pp. 11–12](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/i6b417bdqvnz37uq9du89/cal15.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=llt20vyjqiazl7zk130ogorfj#page=12) · Wiener & Hopf, “Über eine Klasse singulärer Integralgleichungen,” [ADS bibliographic record](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1932ubek.book.....H/abstract). |
| June | **Eisenstein series.** | **Ferdinand Gotthold Max Eisenstein.** Modular forms turn a lattice sum into a holomorphic function with rigid transformation laws; Eisenstein's short life produced major work in both number theory and elliptic functions. | [Eisenstein series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenstein_series) · [Eisenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthold_Eisenstein). |
| July | **Struve special functions.** | **Karl Hermann von Struve.** The Struve functions solve an inhomogeneous Bessel-type equation, making a useful compare/contrast with the calendar's Bessel and Neumann months. | [Struve function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struve_function) · NIST DLMF, Chapter 11. |
| August | **Alice Roth's “Swiss cheese” compact set**, not a single complex function: start with the closed unit disk and remove countably many pairwise disjoint open disks `D(a_j,r_j)` with `Σ r_j<1`. Roth's construction is maximal in the relevant approximation sense. The calendar relaxes the radius constraint visually so the holes can be seen. Render the set formation itself rather than inventing a phase portrait. | **Alice Roth.** Her dissertation produced a counterexample in rational approximation; the later historical paper is also useful because her work largely disappeared from standard accounts before being rediscovered. | [Calendar pp. 17–18](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/i6b417bdqvnz37uq9du89/cal15.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=llt20vyjqiazl7zk130ogorfj#page=18) · Daepp, Gauthier, Gorkin & Schmieder, “Alice in Switzerland: The Life and Mathematics of Alice Roth” (2005), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02984813). |
| September | **Gaussian radial-basis-function determinant, not a spline picture.** For 21 data sites, `φ_k(x,y;ε)=exp(-ε^2((x-x_k)^2+(y-y_k)^2))`, form the `21×21` interpolation matrix `A(ε)` and plot `det A(ε)` in the first quadrant of the complex `ε`-plane using the stable RBF-GA reformulation. **Reproduction limit:** the month page does not list the 21 node coordinates, so a new render can reproduce the construction but not claim the exact same determinant portrait without those data. | **Isaac Jacob Schoenberg**, with the month contributed by **Bengt Fornberg**. Schoenberg's positive-definiteness results explain why Gaussian interpolation is nonsingular for distinct real nodes; modern RBF-GA algorithms prevent numerical collapse as the Gaussians become flat. | [Calendar pp. 19–20](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/i6b417bdqvnz37uq9du89/cal15.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=llt20vyjqiazl7zk130ogorfj#page=20) · Fornberg, Larsson & Flyer, “Stable Computations with Gaussian Radial Basis Functions” (2011), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/09076756X). |
| October | **Dedekind eta function `η(τ)`.** | **Richard Dedekind.** An infinite product with modular transformation laws connects partitions, modular forms and complex lattices. | [Dedekind eta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind_eta_function) · [Richard Dedekind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dedekind). |
| November | **Gravitational lensing as zeros of harmonic rational maps.** | **Sun Hong Rhie.** Rhie constructed point-mass lenses with unexpectedly many images, helping settle the sharp image-count problem; this is one of the strongest “physical picture becomes complex function” calendar stories. | [Gravitational microlensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_microlensing) · Rhie, “n-point gravitational lenses with 5(n−1) images,” arXiv:astro-ph/0305166; Khavinson & Neumann on zeros of rational harmonic functions. |
| December | **Riemann surface of `Log(cos(z^5-z^-5))`.** The numerical picture uses the principal logarithm. Its visible jump curves are the preimages, under `g(z)=cos(z^5-z^-5)`, of the negative real axis; they terminate at zeros of `g`. A stronger video can begin with the principal branch, then continue analytically across one cut to show why the natural object is an infinite-sheeted Riemann surface rather than a single cut plane. | **Hermann Weyl.** The page connects Riemann's 1851 introduction of Riemann surfaces with Weyl's 1913 axiomatic/topological treatment, which put the idea on a more rigorous footing. | [Calendar pp. 25–26](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/i6b417bdqvnz37uq9du89/cal15.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=llt20vyjqiazl7zk130ogorfj#page=26) · Riemann's 1851 thesis · Weyl, *Die Idee der Riemannschen Fläche* (1913), [digitized first edition](https://archive.org/details/dieideederrieman00weyluoft). |

## Source-audit result

The former February–May, August–September and December picture placeholders are now tied to the calendar's actual construction and source trail. The only remaining limitations are parameters the calendar itself does not print, such as the September node coordinates. Those limits are recorded explicitly; the PDF and artwork remain external and the repository contains only research notes for original renders.
