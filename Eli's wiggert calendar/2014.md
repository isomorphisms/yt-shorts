# Complex Beauties 2014 — source-audit lesson seeds

Primary source: Wegert, Semmler, Gorkin & Daepp, *Complex Beauties 2014*, DOI [10.13140/2.1.2864.3527](https://doi.org/10.13140/2.1.2864.3527), plus the [official archive](https://blogs.hrz.tu-freiberg.de/mathekalender/english/). The calendar PDF and artwork remain external. These notes describe the pictured mathematics so it can be independently rendered; when the calendar itself omits numerical data needed for an exact reconstruction, that limitation is stated explicitly.

| Month | Function / lesson direction | Person / historical story | Reading and citation trail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | **Degree-12 Lagrange interpolation polynomial.** For 13 nodes `z_j` with values `w_j`, `p(z)=Σ_j w_j Π_{k≠j}(z-z_k)/(z_j-z_k)`. The calendar portrait uses 13 data pairs, but does not print them, so its exact polynomial cannot be reconstructed from the explanation alone. A video can animate how each Lagrange basis polynomial switches on one prescribed value while vanishing at the other nodes. | **Edward Waring.** The calendar uses Waring's 1779 interpolation paper rather than Waring's problem in additive number theory; Lagrange published the formula later and Euler also used it. | [Calendar pp. 3–4](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/m6b4p95tzwbduv616qq36/cal14.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=g7dh347shtsigiw2u8fxb5qu6#page=4) · Waring, “Problems concerning interpolations” (1779), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstl.1779.0008). |
| February | **`f(z)=exp(-z^6)-1` and a non-elementary antiderivative.** The point is not Liouville's bounded-entire-function theorem: the page uses Liouville's criterion for when an elementary function has an elementary antiderivative. The phase portrait is of the entire transcendental integrand. | **Joseph Liouville.** His integration theorem explains why an antiderivative can exist analytically while still being impossible to express by a finite combination of elementary operations, exponentials and logarithms. | [Calendar pp. 5–6](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/m6b4p95tzwbduv616qq36/cal14.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=g7dh347shtsigiw2u8fxb5qu6#page=6) · Wegert, *Visual Complex Functions*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-0348-0180-5). The calendar gives no primary Liouville citation for this criterion. |
| March | **Newton iteration**, including Simpson's early use of Newton's method for systems. | **Thomas Simpson.** Simpson's 1740 *Essays in Mathematicks* applied Newton-style iteration beyond a single polynomial equation, long before modern numerical-analysis language. | [Newton's method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton%27s_method) · historical work on Simpson's extension of Newton iteration. |
| April | **Tenth continued-fraction approximant to `tanh z`.** The calendar writes `tanh z=z/(1+z^2/(3+z^2/(5+...)))`; truncate after the first ten odd denominators and evaluate from the bottom upward. A good video can increase the depth and compare the rational zero/pole pattern with the limiting meromorphic `tanh`. | **Carl Friedrich Gauss.** The page presents Gauss's general continued-fraction machinery while noting that Lambert already knew this particular `tanh` fraction in 1768. | [Calendar pp. 9–10](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/m6b4p95tzwbduv616qq36/cal14.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=g7dh347shtsigiw2u8fxb5qu6#page=10) · the calendar points back to its March/July 2013 continued-fraction entries; it gives no title for Lambert's 1768 source. |
| May | **Hadamard gap series** `f(z)=Σ_{j≥0} z^(2^j)`, rendered as the eleven-term partial sum `f_10(z)=Σ_{j=0}^{10} z^(2^j)`. The powers `1,2,4,8,...` leave increasingly huge gaps; the infinite function has the unit circle as a natural boundary. | **Jacques Hadamard.** The relevant story is the Ostrowski–Hadamard gap theorem, not Hadamard factorization. | [Calendar pp. 11–12](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/m6b4p95tzwbduv616qq36/cal14.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=g7dh347shtsigiw2u8fxb5qu6#page=12) · [Hadamard gap theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrowski%E2%80%93Hadamard_gap_theorem). The calendar names the theorem but does not give an original paper. |
| June | **Singular inner function with five boundary atoms.** The page explicitly displays the one-atom model `S(z)=exp((z+1)/(z-1))` and says the monthly function has five discontinuities at the fifth roots of unity. In general it has the form `exp(Σ_j α_j (z+ω_j)/(z-ω_j))`, `ω_j^5=1`, `α_j>0`. **Reproduction limit:** the five weights are not printed, so do not claim an equal-weight render is the exact calendar image. | **Arne Beurling.** The page connects Hardy spaces, inner–outer factorization and Beurling's invariant-subspace work to singular inner functions. | [Calendar pp. 13–14](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/m6b4p95tzwbduv616qq36/cal14.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=g7dh347shtsigiw2u8fxb5qu6#page=14) · G. H. Hardy, “The Mean Value of the Modulus of an Analytic Function” (1915), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s2_14.1.269). |
| July | **Mittag-Leffler function `E_α(z)`** and its interpolation between exponential-like and more slowly growing behavior. | **Gösta Mittag-Leffler.** The namesake special function came from his work on summability/analytic continuation and later became central in fractional differential equations. | [Mittag-Leffler function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittag-Leffler_function) · Gorenflo et al., *Mittag-Leffler Functions, Related Topics and Applications*. |
| August | **Trapezoidal approximation to Schwarz's integral formula.** The boundary datum `h` is `1` on five arcs of the unit circle and `0` on the complementary arcs; the calendar uses ten grid points on each active arc and sums rational kernels `(ζ+z)/(ζ-z)`. **Reproduction limit:** the five arc endpoints, full node list, weights and `Im f(0)` are not printed. A video can still show how adding boundary quadrature nodes creates a rational approximation with poles on the circle. | **Hermann Amandus Schwarz.** This is Schwarz's integral formula for reconstructing an analytic function from its real part on the boundary, following Poisson's solution of the Dirichlet problem. | [Calendar pp. 17–18](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/m6b4p95tzwbduv616qq36/cal14.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=g7dh347shtsigiw2u8fxb5qu6#page=18) · [Schwarz integral formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarz_integral_formula). The calendar supplies no primary paper citation. |
| September | **Digamma function** `ψ(z)=Γ'(z)/Γ(z)`. Its simple poles occur at `0,-1,-2,...`, with a real zero between successive poles. This replaces the earlier Stern–Brocot inference: the calendar's pictured function is the logarithmic derivative of Gamma. | **Moritz Abraham Stern.** Stern is the featured mathematician, but the page does **not** claim he originated the digamma function; the biography connects him to Gauss, Klein and Riemann. | [Calendar pp. 19–20](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/m6b4p95tzwbduv616qq36/cal14.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=g7dh347shtsigiw2u8fxb5qu6#page=20) · [Digamma function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digamma_function) · the calendar points back to August 2012 for `Γ`. |
| October | **Frostman shift of an atomic singular inner function.** With `S(z)=exp((-1+i z)/(1+i z))` and real `a=0.727`, the pictured function is `B(z)=(0.727-S(z))/(1-0.727 S(z))`. Its infinitely many zeros solve `S(z)=0.727`; setting `a=0` removes the zeros and leaves the singular inner function. | **Otto Frostman.** The page uses Frostman's theorem to turn a singular inner function into an infinite Blaschke product by a disk automorphism. | [Calendar pp. 21–22](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/m6b4p95tzwbduv616qq36/cal14.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=g7dh347shtsigiw2u8fxb5qu6#page=22) · Frostman, “Sur les produits de Blaschke,” *Kungliga Fysiografiska Sällskapets i Lund Förhandlingar* 12(15) (1942), 169–182. No stable primary full-text link was verified, so none is invented here. |
| November | **Painlevé transcendents.** Guest contribution credited in the 2014 calendar introduction to Bengt Fornberg. | **Paul Painlevé.** Classifying second-order ODEs by movable singularities produced six new transcendental families; modern asymptotic and numerical work treats them as nonlinear special functions. | [Painlevé transcendents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painlev%C3%A9_transcendents) · Ince, *Ordinary Differential Equations*; modern numerical work by Fornberg and collaborators. |
| December | **Runge-style rational approximation of colored regions.** For the reproducible unit-disk model, let `c=exp(0.625·2πi)`, place poles `b_k=exp(2πik/n)` and interlaced zeros `a_k=exp((k+0.625)2πi/n)`, then use `f_n(z)=Π_k(a_k-z)/(b_k-z)`; the inset uses `n=20`. Multiplying such factors approximates multicolor regions. **Reproduction limit:** the calendar does not print the exact tree/star/candle boundaries or each `n`, so those composite pictures cannot be reconstructed pixel-for-pixel from the text. | **Carl David Tolmé Runge.** Runge's approximation theorem becomes an image-making device: zeros and poles placed around a boundary force nearly constant phase inside and outside. | [Calendar pp. 25–26](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/m6b4p95tzwbduv616qq36/cal14.pdf?dl=0&rlkey=g7dh347shtsigiw2u8fxb5qu6#page=26) · Runge, “Zur Theorie der Eindeutigen Analytischen Functionen” (1885), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02400416) · Wegert, “Complex Functions and Images” (2013), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40315-013-0007-1). |

## Source-audit result

The former exact-picture placeholders are now replaced by source-grounded constructions. Some calendar pages themselves omit the interpolation nodes, singular-inner weights, Schwarz quadrature data, or decorative image boundaries; those are reproduction gaps, not topic-identification gaps. The PDF and artwork stay external, while these descriptions are sufficient for original renders that do not pretend to copy omitted data.
