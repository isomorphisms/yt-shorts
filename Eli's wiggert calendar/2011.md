# Complex Beauties 2011 — lesson seeds

Primary source: [TU Freiberg's official *Complex Beauties* archive](https://blogs.hrz.tu-freiberg.de/mathekalender/english/). The calendar PDF and artwork stay external; this file records original research/lesson notes only.

| Month | Function / pictured object | Person / historical story | Reading and citation trail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | **Polynomial `z^17-1`** — its 17 roots form a regular polygon. | **Carl Friedrich Gauss.** The regular 17-gon was Gauss's spectacular 1796 constructibility result; the same picture is also a clean route into the fundamental theorem of algebra. | [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_Gauss) · [Heptadecagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heptadecagon) · Gauss, *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae* (1801). |
| February | **Rational/meromorphic function** with many nearby zero-pole pairs. | **Gösta Mittag-Leffler.** Rational partial fractions lead naturally to the Mittag-Leffler theorem: prescribe principal parts at poles and build a meromorphic function around them. | [Mittag-Leffler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6sta_Mittag-Leffler) · [Mittag-Leffler's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittag-Leffler%27s_theorem). |
| March | **Taylor polynomial of `1/(1-z)`**, `1+z+...+z^60`; its zeros sit on the unit circle. | **Brook Taylor.** A finite Taylor approximation develops a global zero pattern that the real-variable formula does not advertise. This is a natural bridge to Jentzsch's theorem. | [Brook Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brook_Taylor) · [Taylor series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_series) · [Jentzsch theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jentzsch_theorem). |
| April | **Complex cosine** `cos z`, shown with modified phase coloring. | **Leonhard Euler.** Euler's exponential formula turns trigonometry into complex exponentials; the phase portrait makes that identification geometric. | [Euler's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler%27s_formula) · Euler, *Introductio in analysin infinitorum* (1748). |
| May | **Principal complex logarithm** and the failure of `Log(e^z)=z` globally. | **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz.** The old Bernoulli–Leibniz–Euler controversy over logarithms of negative numbers is a good story about why branches are mathematics, not notation trivia. | [Complex logarithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_logarithm) · [Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz). |
| June | **Cauchy integral formula**, numerically approximated by a rational function whose poles lie on the integration curve. | **Augustin-Louis Cauchy.** Contour integration converts boundary values into an analytic function inside; quadrature converts that theorem into a rational approximation you can see. | [Cauchy's integral formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy%27s_integral_formula) · Cauchy's nineteenth-century memoirs on complex integration. |
| July | **`exp(1/z^2)`**, an essential singularity. | **Charles Émile Picard.** Near an essential singularity a function takes almost every complex value infinitely often; the phase portrait makes the violent local behavior immediate. | [Picard theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_theorem) · [Essential singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_singularity). |
| August | **`exp(sin z + i z)`** with saddle/steepest-descent geometry. | **Pierre-Simon Laplace.** Laplace's asymptotic method became the saddle-point/steepest-descent method: deform attention toward the places that dominate an integral. | [Laplace's method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplace%27s_method) · [Method of steepest descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_steepest_descent). |
| September | **Jacobi elliptic `dn`**, a doubly periodic meromorphic function. | **Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi.** Inverting elliptic integrals produces elliptic functions; two independent periods mean the plane is secretly repeating on a lattice. | [Jacobi elliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobi_elliptic_functions) · Jacobi, *Fundamenta nova theoriae functionum ellipticarum* (1829). |
| October | **Weierstrass elliptic `℘` and `℘'`** on a lattice. | **Karl Weierstrass.** The `℘` function is the canonical lattice-built elliptic function; poles and periodicity are especially legible in Wegert coloring. | [Weierstrass elliptic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_elliptic_function) · [Elliptic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_function). |
| November | **Riemann zeta function `ζ(s)`** and its zeros. | **Bernhard Riemann.** Riemann's 1859 memoir moved Euler's zeta function into the complex plane and tied its zeros to the distribution of primes. | [Riemann zeta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_zeta_function) · Riemann, *Ueber die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Grösse* (1859). |
| December | **Complex potential / rational field generated by point charges.** | **James Clerk Maxwell.** Analytic functions package two-dimensional potential theory: level curves and field lines become the real and imaginary geometry of one complex function. | [Complex potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_potential) · Maxwell, *A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism* (1873). |

## Video directions

Best early experiments: grow the March Taylor polynomial and watch its zeros fill the unit circle; tile the September/October plane to expose double periodicity; zoom into July's essential singularity; and animate the May branch cut while comparing `Log(e^z)` with `z`.
