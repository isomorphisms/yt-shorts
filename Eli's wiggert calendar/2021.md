# Complex Beauties 2021 — lesson seeds

Primary source: Wegert, Semmler, Gorkin & Daepp, *Complex Beauties 2021*, author-uploaded full text; [official archive](https://blogs.hrz.tu-freiberg.de/mathekalender/english/). Calendar assets remain external.

| Month | Function / pictured object | Person / historical story | Reading and citation trail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | **Laguerre polynomial**, pictured as `e^{-z/2}L_8(z)`. | **Edmond Laguerre.** Orthogonal polynomials arising from a differential equation become radial wavefunctions in quantum mechanics; the phase portrait makes their eight positive real zeros conspicuous. | [Laguerre polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguerre_polynomials) · NIST DLMF Chapter 18. |
| February | **d'Alembert solution of the 1D wave equation**, visualized through a complex wave such as `exp(iu)`. | **Jean le Rond d'Alembert.** Traveling waves split naturally into left- and right-moving pieces, making PDE evolution a shift rather than a mysterious global recomputation. | [D'Alembert's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%27Alembert%27s_formula) · [Wave equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_equation). |
| March | **Plane waves and their superposition in two dimensions.** | **Daniel Bernoulli.** The eighteenth-century dispute over vibrating strings led to the principle that complicated motion can be decomposed into simple modes—an ancestor of Fourier analysis. | [Superposition principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superposition_principle) · [Daniel Bernoulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Bernoulli). |
| April | **Hankel determinant**, the calendar using a `3×3` determinant built from function values/coefficients. | **Hermann Hankel.** Constant-antidiagonal matrices and their determinants encode moment sequences, rational approximation and recurrence structure. | [Hankel matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hankel_matrix) · [Moment problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_problem). |
| May | **Faddeeva function** `w(z)=e^{-z^2}erfc(-iz)`. | **Vera Faddeeva.** A function developed for numerical tables now appears in plasma dispersion, spectroscopy and stable evaluation of complex error functions. | [Faddeeva function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faddeeva_function) · NIST DLMF §7.9. |
| June | **Poisson point process** used to place random zeros and poles in the complex plane. | **Ernst Abbe.** The historical route is from Abbe's counting/statistical ideas to Poisson processes; the visual lesson is a random meromorphic function assembled from a random point pattern. | [Poisson point process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_point_process) · Guttorp & Thorarinsdottir, *International Statistical Review* 80(2) (2012); H. Paul Keeler's survey notes. |
| July | **Wiman conjecture counterexample / growth of entire functions**, calendar front showing a finite product truncation `f_7`. | **Walter K. Hayman.** Wiman predicted a strong relation between maximum and minimum modulus; Hayman constructed counterexamples, turning an abstract growth question into a visible product of zeros. | [Walter Hayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Hayman) · literature on Wiman's conjecture and Hayman's counterexamples. |
| August | **Ruscheweyh derivatives**, pictured as `D^5 tan z`. | **Stephan Ruscheweyh.** A differential-convolution operator used in geometric function theory transforms familiar functions into new univalent/convexity test objects. | [Geometric function theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_function_theory) · Wegert, “The Ruscheweyh Derivatives: To the Memory of Stephan Ruscheweyh,” *Comput. Methods Funct. Theory*. |
| September | **Random power series.** | **Jean-Pierre Kahane.** Random coefficients turn questions about convergence, boundary behavior and zeros into probability; deterministic analytic structure survives inside randomness. | [Random analytic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_analytic_function) · Kahane, *Some Random Series of Functions*, 2nd ed. (1985); Barral, Peyrière & Queffélec, *EMS Newsletter* 108 (2018). |
| October | **Schröder functional equation**, using `φ(z)=2z/(1+z^2)` and `f(z)=arctan(iz)`; calendar shows `φ^3`. | **Ernst Schröder.** Conjugating an iteration to multiplication can linearize nonlinear dynamics near a fixed point. | [Schröder's equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schr%C3%B6der%27s_equation) · [Koenigs function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koenigs_function). |
| November | **2D Ising correlation function expressed through Painlevé III**, continued to another Riemann sheet. Guest contribution by Marco Fasondini. | **Ernst Ising.** A lattice spin model that Ising could solve only in one dimension later became one of the central exactly solved models; its correlations unexpectedly meet nonlinear special functions. | [Ising model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ising_model) · [Painlevé transcendents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painlev%C3%A9_transcendents) · classical McCoy–Tracy–Wu work. |
| December | **Padé poles revealing a natural branch cut / Stahl compact set**, using a high-degree rational approximant to a multivalued square-root function. | **Herbert Stahl.** Stahl proved where near-diagonal Padé approximants converge for multivalued analytic functions: poles organize themselves along a minimal-capacity cut. | [Padé approximant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pad%C3%A9_approximant) · Aptekarev, Nevai & Totik, *J. Approx. Theory* 183 (2014), A1–A26; Stahl's convergence papers. |

## Video directions

May can compare `erf`, `erfc` and `w(z)`; June can literally drop random zeros/poles into the plane; October can show nonlinear iteration becoming multiplication after conjugacy; December is a strong animation—raise Padé degree and watch poles draw the branch cut for you.
