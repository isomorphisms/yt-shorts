# Trigonometry — visual Short notebook

This folder is a source and experiment notebook for **original visual YouTube Shorts about trigonometry**. The entries are not finished scripts. The useful unit here is a concrete situation where changing an angle visibly changes something else.

## First queue

- [Laser pointer on a wall](laser-pointer-tangent.md) — equal angular motion produces rapidly increasing motion of the spot as the beam becomes nearly parallel to the wall; the basic geometry is `x = d tan θ`.
- [Squaring a shed](squaring-a-shed.md) — turn a small angular error at a corner into a measurable mismatch between the two diagonals.
- [Cars on a road at different angles](cars-at-road-angles.md) — keep the cars at 55 mph and rotate the road away from perpendicular to the viewer; separate the across-view and toward/away components.
- [Electricity](electricity.md) — practical sine/cosine examples from AC voltage, phase difference, and power factor.
- [Albrecht Dürer](albrecht-durer.md) — geometry, measurement, and perspective constructions from *Underweysung der Messung*; use the historical diagrams as sources and reconstruct the geometry cleanly.
- [Eli Maor — Trigonometric Delights](trigonometric-delights-eli-maor.md) — chapter queue for later source-grounded Shorts rather than one generic “history of trigonometry” video.

## Production rule

Prefer one relationship per Short. Put the physical or geometric situation on screen first, then reveal the triangle/angle that controls it. Whenever possible let the viewer see the quantity change before showing a graph.

For applied examples, keep the physical speed, length, voltage, or rotation rate fixed while changing only the angle. That makes the trigonometric dependence visible instead of burying it in several simultaneous changes.

## Reusable visual vocabulary

- rotate one ray while keeping the other geometry fixed;
- leave ghost marks at 10° increments so nonlinear spacing is visible;
- decompose one motion/vector into perpendicular components;
- draw the right triangle only after the physical setup is understandable;
- show a real measurement beside the ideal geometric prediction;
- for a construction example, exaggerate the error first and then shrink it to a realistic value;
- keep an exact formula available, but do not make the formula the opening image.
