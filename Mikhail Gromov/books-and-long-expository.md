# Mikhail Gromov — books and long expository sources

Use these as mines. A page can contain more than one Short; a chapter can contain dozens.

## Sign and Geometric Meaning of Curvature

M. Gromov, *Rendiconti del Seminario Matematico e Fisico di Milano* 61 (1991), 9–123.  
Official page: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/expository/34/  
Direct PDF: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/177.pdf

This is the first source to mine. Gromov says at the beginning that the monograph tries to reveal the development of Riemannian geometry to noninitiates by following relatively few ideas. The first ten PDF pages alone supply a chain of visual fragments: derivative signs and convexity, second fundamental form as departure from a tangent plane, equidistant offsets, curvature of offsets, nearest-point projection, singular inward offsets, and mean curvature/volume growth.

See the page-level audit in [sign-and-geometric-meaning-of-curvature.md](sign-and-geometric-meaning-of-curvature.md).

## Metric Structures for Riemannian and Non-Riemannian Spaces

M. Gromov, Birkhäuser, 1999.  
Official page/PDF: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/distancegeometry/112/

This is too large to reduce to a topic list and call the job done. Search it for individual metric constructions and examples. Candidate lanes include comparison of metric spaces, approximation/convergence, systolic/filling ideas and quantitative topology, but each candidate needs an exact page audit before becoming a Short note.

The 1999 English book expands the earlier French **Structures métriques pour les variétés riemanniennes** (1981), written/redacted with J. Lafontaine and P. Pansu.

## Partial Differential Relations

M. Gromov, Springer-Verlag, 1986, Ergebnisse der Mathematik, 3rd series, vol. 9.  
Official page: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/hprinciple/94/

Possible lane: make the distinction between “rigid equation to solve” and “differential relation with room to deform” visible before saying anything sweeping about the h-principle. A later extraction can show a concrete relation and a deformation family.

Status: bibliography verified; visual fragments need page-by-page audit.

## Carnot-Carathéodory Spaces Seen from Within

M. Gromov, in *Sub-Riemannian Geometry*, Progress in Mathematics 144 (1996), pp. 79–323.

Strong potential for pictures: permitted tangent directions, shortest paths under directional constraints, metric balls that are visibly non-Euclidean. Exact passages still need a source/page audit before scripting.

## Four Lectures on Scalar Curvature

M. Gromov, 2019.  
Official page: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/expository/794/  
Direct PDF: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/scalar-lectures-IHES-2019-6.pdf

The beginning includes an elementary-background run before the later scalar-curvature machinery. Candidate visual fragments:

- equidistant hypersurfaces;
- small metric balls and how their volume departs from the Euclidean model;
- scaling a metric and watching curvature rescale;
- second variation and nearby geodesics.

Status: broad source region checked; exact fragment locators should be added as they are selected.

## Reading strategy

A useful Short from Gromov should normally stop **earlier** than a textbook explanation would. If the source gives a striking geometric equivalence, deformation, extremal question, or counterintuitive picture, expose that object first. Link the source. Add the theorem only when the picture really supports the statement.
