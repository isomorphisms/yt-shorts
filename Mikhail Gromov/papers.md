# Mikhail Gromov — papers to mine

This is a first index of major papers with possible visual doors. The visual door is not the theorem; page-audit the exact statement before writing narration.

## 1981 — Groups of polynomial growth and expanding maps

M. Gromov, *Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS* 53 (1981), 53–73.  
Official page/PDF: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/infinitegroups/147/

Picture door:

- draw a Cayley graph from a small generating set;
- count the vertices in balls of radius 1, 2, 3, …;
- put the counts next to familiar linear/quadratic/exponential growth curves;
- only after the growth notion is visible mention how deep the classification theorem is.

This can probably support several Shorts: “ball in a Cayley graph,” “what polynomial growth means,” and later “why nilpotent groups appear.”

## 1982 — Volume and bounded cohomology

M. Gromov, *Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS* 56 (1982), 5–100.  
Official page/PDF: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/metricinvariants/117/

Possible lane: simplicial volume and the idea that a manifold's fundamental cycle can be represented with a controlled total coefficient mass. This requires a careful introductory example; do not lead with the full bounded-cohomology formalism.

Status: source verified; visual entry point needs page audit.

## 1983 — Filling Riemannian manifolds

M. Gromov, *Journal of Differential Geometry* 18 (1983), 1–147.  
Author page/PDF: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/metricinvariants/118/  
Journal page: https://projecteuclid.org/journals/journal-of-differential-geometry/volume-18/issue-1/Filling-Riemannian-manifolds/10.4310/jdg/1214509283.full

Promising separate fragments:

- a shortest noncontractible loop as a geometric scale;
- “filling” a boundary by an interior;
- filling radius as a measure of how far a space must extend before it can be filled in an ambient space;
- the tension between short essential loops and total volume;
- surfaces as the low-dimensional picture before the general theorem.

The paper is long enough that these should become different notes, not one summary.

## 1985 — Pseudo-holomorphic curves in symplectic manifolds

M. Gromov, *Inventiones Mathematicae* 82 (1985), 307–347.  
Official page/PDF: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/symplecticmanifolds/160/

Potential entry point: start with an almost-complex structure as a rule for rotating tangent directions and then show a surface whose tangent planes respect that rule. This is more prerequisite-heavy than the metric/curvature material and should probably come later.

## 1987 — Hyperbolic groups

M. Gromov, “Hyperbolic groups,” in S. Gersten (ed.), *Essays in Group Theory*, MSRI Publications 8 (1987), 75–265.  
Official page/PDF: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/infinitegroups/149/

Strong picture doors:

- geodesic triangles that are thin rather than Euclidean-fat;
- a tree as the extreme model where a geodesic triangle collapses to a tripod;
- Cayley graphs viewed from farther and farther away;
- compare Euclidean grid growth with a negatively curved / hyperbolic-looking graph.

“Thin triangle” deserves its own Short before any group-theoretic consequences.

## 2003 — Isoperimetry of waists and concentration of maps

M. Gromov, *Geometric and Functional Analysis* 13 (2003), 178–215.  
Official page/PDF: https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/metricmeasureconcentration/174/

Possible picture door: map a sphere or other high-dimensional object to a lower-dimensional target and ask how small every fiber can be. The “waist” idea has a strong visual analogy, but use the exact theorem only after fixing dimension, measure, and neighborhood conventions from the paper.
