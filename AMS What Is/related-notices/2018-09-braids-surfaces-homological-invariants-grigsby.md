## Small mathematical units visible in the source

### 1. A knot versus a surface it bounds

Grigsby begins from knots and links in `S^3` and shifts attention to smoothly embedded surfaces they bound. That move is visually simple: the boundary knot can stay fixed while different candidate spanning surfaces appear.

### 2. Slice versus ribbon

The article uses Fox’s slice-ribbon question as motivation. This is naturally drawable as a comparison between a disk in the 4-ball and the more constrained ribbon picture, provided the eventual animation does not pretend a 4-dimensional embedding is literally visible in 3-space.

### 3. Braids as controlled knot diagrams

Braids give a rigid way to organize knot/link pictures before closing them up. A short visual can show strands, a braid word/change, and the closure without requiring a complete homological construction.

### 4. Homological invariants as modern tools

The source moves from the classical geometric questions to homological invariants arising from quantum topology. For a later source-deep pass, identify one explicit calculation or comparison from the article rather than making an abstract “homology detects topology” claim.

### 5. Torus-knot material

The AMS issue includes a figure involving a **(3,4)-torus knot** and a braided surface bounded by it. This is the concrete torus cue behind the memory match.
