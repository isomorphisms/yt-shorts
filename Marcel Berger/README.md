# Marcel Berger

Source mine for short, picture-first geometry pieces.

The aim here is not to summarize a whole book or paper in one Short. A chapter can contain dozens of usable fragments. Record the smallest interesting mathematical move that can be shown cleanly, keep the original locator, and leave the full source available for someone who wants to read further.

## Working rule

For each candidate fragment, preserve:

- exact source and stable link;
- page/section/figure when it has actually been checked;
- one mathematical claim or question;
- the picture or motion that could expose it;
- prerequisites and caveats;
- status: `verified` if checked in the source, `locator-only` if found from a table of contents/index and still needing a page audit.

Do not turn a suggestive two-dimensional drawing into a claim about a higher-dimensional theorem without saying what the drawing is only illustrating.

## Strong first-pass sources

1. **Geometry Revealed: A Jacob's Ladder to Modern Higher Geometry** (2010). Springer describes it as visually rich; the contents alone give a large catalog of possible Shorts: circles and spheres, distributing points on a sphere, plane curves, convexity, polytopes, lattice packings and tilings, billiards, and geodesic flow.  
   Source: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-540-70997-8
2. **A Panoramic View of Riemannian Geometry** (2003). Useful because Berger often motivates a construction before moving to the general theorem.  
   Source: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-18245-7
3. **Geometry I**. Classical/projective/Euclidean geometry gives many low-prerequisite visual fragments: tilings, polyhedra, projective configurations, conics, spheres, elliptic and hyperbolic geometry, convexity.
4. **Problems in Geometry** (with Pierre Pansu, Jean-Pic Berry, Xavier Saint-Raymond). A problem collection is useful for Shorts because a single diagram/question can stand on its own.  
   Source: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-1836-2
5. **Differential Geometry: Manifolds, Curves, and Surfaces** (with Bernard Gostiaux). Curves and surfaces provide direct animation material.  
   Source: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-1033-7

## Paper lanes worth mining

- holonomy and parallel transport;
- positive sectional curvature and pinching;
- systolic geometry, especially *Du côté de chez Pu*;
- spectra/Laplacians;
- variation formulas;
- the later pinching work that explicitly uses Gromov compactness.
