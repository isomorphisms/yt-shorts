# Marcel Berger — books to mine

This is an index, not a claim that one chapter should become one Short. Treat each listed chapter as a region to search for individual diagrams, constructions, examples, questions, or proofs that can be detached without mutilating the mathematics.

## Geometry Revealed: A Jacob's Ladder to Modern Higher Geometry

Marcel Berger, Springer, 2010. 831 pages.  
Source: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-540-70997-8

Springer's table of contents gives unusually good first-pass coordinates:

- **Points and lines in the plane**, pp. 1–59
- **Circles and spheres**, pp. 61–139
- **The sphere by itself: can we distribute points on it evenly?**, pp. 141–180
- **Conics and quadrics**, pp. 181–247
- **Plane curves**, pp. 249–340
- **Smooth surfaces**, pp. 341–407
- **Convexity and convex sets**, pp. 409–504
- **Polygons, polyhedra, polytopes**, pp. 505–561
- **Lattices, packings and tilings in the plane**, pp. 563–622
- **Lattices and packings in higher dimensions**, pp. 623–674
- **Geometry and dynamics I: billiards**, pp. 675–737
- **Geometry and dynamics II: geodesic flow on a surface**, pp. 739–783

### Best regions for picture-sized pieces

- Put a small number of points on a sphere and ask what “evenly spaced” could mean. Then show competing configurations as the number changes.
- Convex hull: begin with a moving set of points and let the smallest convex set containing them appear.
- Packing versus covering versus tiling: keep the same lattice and change the question.
- Billiards: one ray, one boundary collision, then the variational interpretation.
- Geodesic flow: launch nearby directions and show what the surface does to them.

Status: `locator-only` until the exact passages/figures are checked.

## A Panoramic View of Riemannian Geometry

Marcel Berger, Springer, 2003.  
Source: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-18245-7

The opening chapter is especially promising because it starts from old/new Euclidean geometry before the machinery of Riemannian geometry. The publisher contents place that chapter at pp. 1–99. More detailed contents/previews put several useful elementary topics near the beginning.

Candidate fragments to page-audit:

- first variation of length and the billiard/reflection law;
- curvature of a plane curve;
- the four-vertex theorem;
- the transition from Euclidean distance questions to intrinsic geometry;
- later sections on the geometric meaning of sectional and Ricci curvature;
- geodesic flow.

Status: `locator-only`; verify exact internal page numbers against the book before scripting.

## Geometry I

Marcel Berger. Springer/Universitext editions and previews exist; one searchable preview:  
https://books.google.com/books?id=Se1HAAAAQBAJ

Promising indexed topics include:

- plane tilings and crystallographic groups;
- spherical tilings and regular polyhedra;
- Thales, Pappus and Desargues configurations;
- projective lines, cross-ratios and homographies;
- conics and quadrics;
- spheres, triangles and circles;
- elliptic and hyperbolic geometry;
- convex sets;
- the space of spheres.

These are unusually good Short material because many claims can be shown before the general language is introduced.

Status: `locator-only`.

## Differential Geometry: Manifolds, Curves, and Surfaces

Marcel Berger and Bernard Gostiaux, Springer GTM 115, English edition 1988.  
Source: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-1033-7

Search regions:

- local theory of curves;
- global theory of curves;
- local theory of surfaces;
- global theory of surfaces;
- critical points and degree, if a simple geometric example presents itself.

Status: `locator-only`.

## Riemannian Geometry During the Second Half of the Twentieth Century

Marcel Berger, University Lecture Series 17, AMS, 2000. This is more useful as a map of what to mine elsewhere than as the first source for animation. It surveys major streams including curvature/topology, distinguished metrics, Laplace spectra and geodesic flow.

Use it to discover names/results, then go to the original paper or a more visual Berger treatment before making a Short.
