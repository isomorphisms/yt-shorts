# 3-folds and canonical singularities

Primary index: https://mreid.warwick.ac.uk/3folds/

This note groups Reid's 3-fold work by idea rather than publication date. It is not a substitute for reading the papers individually.

## Early quadrics

### The complete intersection of two or more quadrics — PhD thesis (1972)

PDF: https://mreid.warwick.ac.uk/3folds/qu.pdf

Status: **index-only / source located**.

Themes to retrieve in a deeper pass:

- geometry of varieties cut out by several quadratic equations;
- pencils of quadrics;
- linear spaces lying on intersections of quadrics;
- relation to hyperelliptic curves and their Jacobians/Prym-type constructions;
- rationality questions.

The thesis is still cited in modern work on intersections of two quadrics, so it is not merely historical.

## Canonical 3-folds and minimal models

Reid's 3-fold page puts the following sequence together:

- *Canonical 3-folds* (1980)
- *Minimal models of canonical 3-folds* (1983)
- *Projective morphisms according to Kawamata* (1983)
- *Tendencious survey of 3-folds* (1987)
- *Young person's guide to canonical singularities* (1987)

This is the core historical cluster around the higher-dimensional minimal model program.

Concept atoms for future source reading:

- canonical versus terminal singularities;
- discrepancies as a measure of how the canonical divisor changes under resolution;
- why mild singularities have to be allowed if one wants minimal models to exist;
- divisorial contractions;
- small contractions;
- flips and flops;
- Q-factoriality;
- index of a singularity and index-one covers;
- quotient singularities as explicit local models;
- weighted blowups;
- the difference between classifying smooth varieties and classifying birational models.

Do not attribute the entire minimal model program to Reid. This cluster belongs to a large body of work involving Mori, Kawamata, Shokurov, Kollár and many others; Reid's papers are important expositions/contributions inside that history.

## What is a flip?

PDF: https://mreid.warwick.ac.uk/3folds/what_flip.pdf

Status: **source-read at introduction level**.

Reid's central explanatory idea is that a flip can arise naturally from geometric invariant theory for a `C*` action.

Concept atoms:

- a birational map that is an isomorphism in codimension one;
- why flips change curves while leaving divisors essentially untouched;
- `C*` actions with positive and negative weights;
- taking different GIT quotients of the same affine object;
- projective space, blowups and flips all described through graded rings / `Proj`-style constructions;
- flips as a change of quotient chamber rather than an arbitrary surgery;
- the affine Gorenstein 4-fold carrying a `C*` action behind a 3-fold flip.

This note is likely a better first source for explaining the *idea* of a flip than a classification paper.

## Fano 3-folds

Reid repeatedly uses Fano 3-folds as explicit test objects for birational geometry.

Useful subclusters:

### Birational rigidity

Corti–Pukhlikov–Reid, *Birationally rigid Fano hypersurfaces*.

Potential idea units:

- a Fano variety can be so rigid that any birational map to another Mori fibre space is forced by obvious automorphisms;
- contrast rationality with birational rigidity;
- maximal singularity methods.

### Weighted hypersurfaces / graded-ring classification

Cross-link: [`graded-rings-unprojection-Fano.md`](graded-rings-unprojection-Fano.md).

### Q-Fano threefolds of index 2

Reid–Prokhorov, arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0852

Index-level topics:

- Fano index;
- Q-Fano singular varieties;
- bounded numerical possibilities;
- explicit classification constraints.

### Fano index at least 3

Reid–Suzuki, listed as work 53 in Reid's current bibliography.

Treat as **index-only** until the final published paper is read.

### Kawamata bounds and the Graded Ring Database

Brown–Kasprzyk–Reid manuscript: https://kasprzyk.work/research/pdf/grdb.pdf

Status: **abstract-read**.

The paper turns Kawamata's boundedness inequality into a computable list of **39,550 candidate Hilbert series** for Fano 3-folds satisfying the stated stability inequality. The candidates are not all asserted to exist.

Concept atoms:

- a qualitative boundedness theorem made computational;
- Hilbert series as fingerprints for polarised varieties;
- candidate versus realised Fano 3-fold;
- extracting expected generator weights from a Hilbert series;
- classification by database as an interface between theorem, computation and construction.
