# McKay correspondence and quotient singularities

Primary Reid resource page: https://mreid.warwick.ac.uk/McKay/

This cluster connects finite-group representations, quotient singularities, resolutions, topology/cohomology, toric geometry and Hilbert schemes.

## Surface cyclic quotients

Reid chapter: https://mreid.warwick.ac.uk/surf/more/cyclic.pdf

Status: **source-read at introduction level**.

For a cyclic group acting diagonally on `C²`, Reid studies singularities of type `1/r(1,a)`.

Idea units:

- quotienting a smooth plane by a finite algebraic symmetry can create a singular point;
- invariant monomials generate the quotient coordinate ring;
- Hirzebruch–Jung continued fractions encode the exceptional curves in a resolution;
- the resolution becomes a chain of rational curves with specified self-intersections;
- these calculations lead naturally into toric geometry;
- the minimal resolution can be viewed as a moduli space of group-clusters.

## Du Val / ADE singularities

Reid chapter: https://mreid.warwick.ac.uk/surf/more/DuVal.pdf

The families `A_n`, `D_n`, `E_6`, `E_7`, `E_8` appear as surface singularities. Their resolutions produce configurations of `(-2)`-curves arranged like ADE Dynkin diagrams. Quotients `C²/G` for finite subgroups of `SL(2,C)` provide another description, and the resolutions are crepant.

Separate concepts:

- the ADE list as singular surfaces;
- exceptional curves and Dynkin diagrams;
- quotient singularities;
- crepant resolution;
- canonical class under resolution;
- numerical cycle;
- the same ADE combinatorics recurring in geometry, group theory and Lie theory.

## Reid's McKay lectures

Miles Reid, *McKay correspondence*: https://arxiv.org/abs/alg-geom/9702016

Status: **abstract-read**.

Reid emphasizes explicit numerical examples and two correspondences:

1. representations of the group ↔ cohomology of a resolution;
2. conjugacy classes ↔ homology of a resolution.

He also discusses mirror symmetry and Nakamura's Hilbert scheme of group-clusters.

## Ito–Reid

Yukari Ito and Miles Reid, *The McKay correspondence for finite subgroups of SL(3,C)*, bibliography item 25: https://mreid.warwick.ac.uk/Personal/works

This moves the classical surface/ADE story into dimension three. Deeper theorem notes should wait for a direct paper read.

## Bridgeland–King–Reid

Tom Bridgeland, Alastair King and Miles Reid, *The McKay correspondence as an equivalence of derived categories*: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9908027

Core retrieval note: the correspondence is strengthened from a numerical matching to a derived-category equivalence. This is joint work and should always be credited to all three authors.

## Craw–Reid: How to calculate A-Hilb C³

Alastair Craw and Miles Reid: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9909085

Publication/abstract page: https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/how-to-calculate-a-hilbc3/

Status: **abstract/result-summary read**.

This is exceptionally visual. Their calculation uses:

- the junior simplex;
- Newton polygons;
- continued fractions;
- regular tessellations by equilateral triangles;
- a toric fan read from the tessellation;
- the toric variety associated to that fan.

Potential small units:

1. Encode a diagonal Abelian action as lattice data in a simplex.
2. Explain why triangulation data describes a toric resolution.
3. Continued fractions control boundary pieces.
4. Regular triangles fill the junior simplex.
5. The tessellation becomes a fan.
6. The fan becomes the resolved variety.

The exact triangle algorithm should be re-read before animation.
